Indian Constitution

The Constitution of India is the supreme law of the nation, acting as the foundation for the governance of the world's largest democracy.
Drafted by a Constituent Assembly led by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar , it was adopted on November 26, 1949, and officially came into effect on January 26, 1950.
Recognized as the longest written constitution globally, it currently contains 448 articles, 25 parts, and 12 schedules.
It establishes India as a sovereign, socialist, secular, and democratic republic, ensuring fundamental rights, justice, and liberty for all its citizens.
